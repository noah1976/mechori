# Raw Trends and Timing: MECHORI
*Skill: startup-positioning | Generated: 2026-09-22*

## Relevant trends

### Records moving from paper to portable digital history

- **Type:** Behavioral / industry digitization
- **Evidence:** CARFAX and Digital Servicebook both make digital service history a core product object; community discussions still show mixed binders, spreadsheets, scans, and phone photos.
- **Strength:** Strong as a category reality; unknown as MECHORI demand.
- **Direction:** Tailwind.
- **Implication:** Buyers can understand a digital record, but MECHORI needs a sharper “used in the next Workshop” outcome.

### Workshop systems and repair information remain separate silos

- **Type:** Workflow / market structure
- **Evidence:** FAINES provides professional repair information; Digital Servicebook emphasizes integration with existing Workshop systems; MECHORI internal strategy explicitly avoids replacing DMS, invoices, and repair manuals at entry.
- **Strength:** Medium.
- **Direction:** Both tailwind and headwind.
- **Implication:** The Owner-controlled handoff can bridge silos, but another workflow step is a serious adoption cost.

### General AI lowers the value of generic explanation

- **Type:** Technology / buyer expectation
- **Evidence:** Internal competitive analysis already treats general AI as a fast explanation alternative.
- **Strength:** Medium; no MECHORI customer comparison yet.
- **Direction:** Headwind for generic knowledge copy; neutral-to-tailwind for longitudinal first-party history.
- **Implication:** Do not lead with “AI answers.” Lead with the vehicle's own carried-forward history and traceable outcomes.

## Trend overlay recommendation

Do not put a trend in the headline. “More vehicle information is becoming digital, but still does not travel cleanly across Owners and Workshops” is supporting context, not the position itself.

## Global boundary

Japan's inspection records, service books, FAINES, and Workshop practices are local evidence. The universal behavior is broader: vehicle history is created by multiple parties and is often fragmented; each market has different official sources, identifiers, rights, and Workshop systems.

## Data gaps

- No measured adoption rates for the target hobby-car segment.
- No evidence that a new Owner-controlled link fits Workshop intake in each launch market.

## Red Flags

- A trend narrative cannot substitute for Workshop adoption evidence.

## Yellow Flags

- Manufacturer-controlled records may reduce the pain for newer mainstream vehicles, reinforcing the hobby/older/unusual-car segment choice.
